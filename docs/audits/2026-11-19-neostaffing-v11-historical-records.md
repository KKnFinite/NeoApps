## Capture and reporting

- `staffing_roster_revisions` records a cutover baseline and the minimum fields used by the live roster. It records changes to employee identity, classification and active state, area name and hierarchy, and assignment membership. There are no notes, contact fields, pay details or Shift Flow fields. Changes and removals are recorded in the same PostgreSQL transaction as the original writes. No-op source changes create no history row.
- `staffing_roster_closes` is keyed by canonical Sort Date Operation. It retains the captured gateway, Sort definition, Sort date, UTC opening and closing boundaries and publication time. The existing worker visits the default Staffing gateway on its regular safety heartbeat. It looks back seven local days, enrolls known operations whose entire window is covered, and seals at most one due operation per pass. It uses the configured window observed at enrollment. For an operation first seen after its window was edited mid-Sort, the service leaves coverage unavailable. Ambiguous DST boundaries are likewise unavailable. Never interpret zero captured placements as a missing roster if an eligible empty Sort was actually sealed.
- The roster becomes final at the configured local Sort close, using successful roster changes whose last journaled mutation was strictly before that boundary. PostgreSQL does not supply commit timestamps here. A transaction whose actual database commit lands after close can still be attributed before close if all of its roster mutations occurred beforehand. A transaction recording mutations on both sides of the boundary is excluded in its entirety. The worker defers while a roster write transaction is open, so rolled-back changes cannot enter a sealed roster. Publication is atomic and may follow the boundary after the next safety tick or retry.
- `staffing_roster_placements` keeps employee identity and classification and the captured work-area path. It uses the operational attendance-roster population, including active non-management employees regardless of whether they have a mark. Later transfers and edits never rewrite it. Multiple legitimate FT Combo Sort placements are displayed in their own areas. The report deduplicates people in the assigned headcount. An attendance mark without a matching close placement remains attendance evidence only.
- `staffing_attendance_outcomes` retains the latest authorized recorded status per employee and operation, original area, employee identity, available classification, and recorded and updated timestamps. It identifies the source kind and, when available, the source row ID. An explicit clear leaves a status-free row so an older source cannot reappear. Daily-detail rollover copies in-scope linked marks in the same transaction before deletion. The cutover import handles both surviving legacy sources under the checks below. No notes, work-hours segments or inferred HERE are stored. Existing corrections and attendance authorization still apply.
- Reports are read-only and use current authorized area scope intersected with the recorded work area. Named rows retain the existing manager/Master boundary. Employee History uses the captured name or ID where available and applies classification to the retained outcome. It shows the Sort next to the date so two Combo marks on one day remain distinct. Daily Staffing labels complete, partial and unavailable evidence. With history enabled, V11 uses only captured and verified copied outcomes. Original attendance and timecard screens continue to follow their existing source retention.

**Earliest coverage.** The new complete roster history can start with the first eligible Sort opening after an approved cutover, once that Sort is sealed. The report can show the earliest retained linked mark and earliest sealed roster date for its scope. There is no verified complete production date. Older linked marks may be recoverable from the surviving sources. Missing statuses and rosters remain unavailable.

## Approved retention policy and gates

The approved reporting horizon is **nine calendar months**. Subtract nine months from the gateway-local date, clamp month-end to the last real day of the resulting month, and preserve the cutoff date itself. Compare the cutoff to the canonical Sort Date Operation date stamped on each retained row. An overnight Night Sort always ages by its opening date, regardless of the local date of its close, seal, correction or import. For example, on November 30, 2026, the cutoff is February 28, 2026. February 27 expires and the Night Sort dated February 28 remains in scope even if its window closes March 1.

The report window and new outcome writes follow the cutoff whenever the history feature is enabled. Physical cleanup separately requires both `NEOSTAFFING_HISTORY_ENABLED` and `NEOSTAFFING_HISTORY_PURGE_ENABLED`. Both default off. At most 250 outcomes, 250 placements, 250 empty manifests and 250 superseded journal revisions are deleted on a safety pass. A roster manifest is marked unavailable in the same transaction as its first placement removal. The report reads a manifest and its placements together. An interrupted batch rolls back and the existing scheduler retries on a later safety tick. PostgreSQL uses a nonblocking transaction lock for concurrent purge attempts and skips journal compaction if staffing writes or sealing hold the roster lock.

Journal compaction keeps the baseline, the latest predecessor per entity including deletion tombstones, all changes in the retained window, and every event of a change set that straddles the compaction floor. The conservative UTC floor is earlier than the first retained local date and moves earlier for a pending retained seal if necessary. Active predecessor identities can therefore be older than nine months. Those rows are internal reconstruction inputs, separate from published Sort histories.

At the same illustrative volume of 400 people per operating day, 260 days/year, and 15% additional Combo placements, nine months holds roughly 90,000 placement rows and up to 90,000 outcome rows. At 250–400 bytes per row including indexes that is approximately **43–69 MiB** before journal rows, WAL and bloat. Actual storage, legal record classification and hold requirements remain unverified. Source timecard, attendance, summary, and payroll retention policies are unchanged.

## Legacy eligibility audit

- **Daily attendance.** Requires a real employee, a valid status, the explicitly linked operation and matching canonical date, a valid Sort identity and an actual recorded work area. Do not look up a different area from current assignments. An exact daily record takes precedence over a same-employee timecard. If the original timecard label evidence is absent, use the stable employee link for display and leave classification unknown.
- **Timecard only.** Requires matching operation, Sort, day and work area, original linked employee and identity labels, and an initial valid recorded status plus a current edit that corroborates the stored status. If the current edit explicitly clears the record, retain only a status-free marker. A verifiable actorless first edit can establish the state history with unknown classification. A known first actor may support a classification present in the original snapshot.
- **Withheld.** Unlinked detail, mismatched dates or Sorts, absent or conflicting edit evidence, malformed identities and ambiguous duplicate individual rows. Records outside the nine-month window are excluded before eligibility is evaluated. Aggregates, receipt hashes, notes and assignment rows are never used to manufacture individual outcomes or roster snapshots.
- The dry run produces counts, earliest eligible mark and withholding reasons without employee identifiers. It is safe before the new tables exist. The explicit importer copies only into the outcomes table and never replaces an existing record or clear. Source rows, hashes and archives remain as found. Production legacy counts will be known only after the separately approved read-only inventory.

## Migration and rollout for approval

1. Obtain approval to run `python scripts/initialize_staffing_reporting_history.py --audit-legacy-marks` against the deliberately selected PostgreSQL `DATABASE_URL`. The audit runs in a repeatable-read, read-only transaction. Reconcile eligible dates, withheld reasons and data ownership before approving the actual copy.
2. Confirm source-table ownership, the schema search path, safe rollback and a quiet cutover window. After deployment and migration approval, `python scripts/initialize_staffing_reporting_history.py --confirm --import-legacy-marks` installs or upgrades the additive schema and optionally copies the audited legacy evidence. Omit `--import-legacy-marks` for schema and journal only. The initial seed and copy share one transaction and use a five-second lock timeout. Restarting the command does not reseed a live journal or overwrite copied outcomes. Ordinary bootstrap can create empty tables. The explicit migration adds provenance columns and a retention index for a database initialized with the first revision.
3. Approve history feature activation separately. Enable capture before the first intended Sort opens and compare sealed placements and marks with canonical data. Check transfers, Combo counts, stale corrections, withheld evidence and worker recovery. Leave `NEOSTAFFING_HISTORY_PURGE_ENABLED` off during this step.
4. Obtain the records owner's confirmation that the new status and roster copies may expire after nine calendar months and confirm any required preservation or holds. Measure the eligible purge inventory and verify backup and recovery in staging. Obtain explicit production activation approval before setting the purge switch. There is no per-record hold implementation in this change. Keep automatic deletion off wherever a hold or other retention requirement has not been resolved.
5. Disable the purge switch to stop further automatic deletes. Disable the main flag to suspend the new report and scheduled writer. The explicitly installed roster journal continues recording until its three triggers are disabled under an approved procedure. Preserve captured rows and treat a journal interruption as an unavailable interval until a fresh verified baseline is established.
